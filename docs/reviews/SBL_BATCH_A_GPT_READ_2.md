# A1 — Leadership style

## **7.5/10 — Close, but don't publish**

The architecture is now basically right. c4 properly tests the CFO's inference without inventing a direction of non-response bias, and c5 deliberately permits different reasoned leadership choices. 

### Remaining blockers

**c1 — “speed was gained at the cost of buy-in.”**

The exhibit establishes immediate unilateral action, not actual lost buy-in. 

Change to:

> the directive approach enabled an immediate decision but **risked** sacrificing managerial input and buy-in.

---

**c2 — “consultation reduced decision error.”**

The case tells us the engineers' evidence changed Liem's proposal. It does not establish that the revised decision was objectively better.

The GOOD is actually safer here: it says the consultation was substantive and Liem updated his position on evidence. 

So c2 and the GOOD have drifted.

Better criterion:

> consultation ensured relevant technical evidence influenced the decision, reducing the risk of proceeding without considering it.

---

**c5 has an outright prose/logical defect.**

It says multiple reasoned styles can earn full marks, then:

> “a candidate who reasons well to a different style would score nothing for it.” 

That says the opposite of what the criterion intends.

This is publication-blocking because it is student-facing marking logic.

---

**“Strongest competing option” still creates a hidden ranking.**

The candidate shouldn't have to guess which alternative the author considers strongest.

Use:

> **a credible material alternative**

instead.

---

### GOOD overreach #1

> “every session held with the 340 staff facing redeployment...” 

Nothing says consultation means sessions with 340 employees.

Consultation could operate through managers, representatives or teams.

Use simply:

> consultation consumes more management time than unilateral direction.

---

### GOOD overreach #2

> “excluded managers become the channel through which a restructure fails.” 

That's too deterministic.

Use:

> excluding line managers increases implementation risk because their operational knowledge and influence are not incorporated.

---

### Approval

**NO — but this is one clean edit pass away.**

I wouldn't rebuild it.

# A2 — Culture

## **5.5/10 — Do not publish**

The biggest problem here is now **criterion/model inconsistency**.

The rubric has explicitly learned several P-N3 lessons, but the supposed golden answer keeps violating them.

---

## c1 — population error remains

Scenario:

> PCG has 1,400 employees; bonuses reward tonnage/fleet utilisation. 

Rubric:

> “incentives shape what 1,400 employees prioritise daily” 

GOOD:

> “incentives govern what 1,400 employees prioritise every working day.” 

The exhibit never says all 1,400 participate in this bonus system.

Either change the exhibit to **“all employee bonuses...”** or constrain the answer to employees covered by the scheme.

Also:

> “rewards nothing that contract warehousing depends on”

is too absolute. Tonnage/utilisation may still be relevant.

The case only proves that **customer satisfaction and solution design aren't rewarded**.

---

## c3 — wrong organisational level

The exhibit describes **management meetings**. 

c3 says commercial matters may lack adequate:

> “board-level air-time.” 

That's not the same entity.

Use:

> senior-management attention / management decision-making airtime.

---

## c4 — imported industry knowledge

The criterion requires:

> “warehousing is a higher-complexity, higher-interdependency service where communication failures are a primary risk.” 

The exhibit does not tell the candidate that.

It may be plausible business knowledge, but P-N3 means it cannot be a required inference.

More exhibit-safe:

> entering warehousing requires new processes and customer-service capabilities, so a review system which rarely captures planning or communication causes may fail to learn from errors relevant to the new strategy.

---

## GOOD directly contradicts c4

The criterion carefully says:

> case says **RARELY**, not never.

The model heading says:

> **Absent learning loops** 

That is literally the prohibited absolute-from-a-hedge shape.

Rename it:

> **Weak learning loops**

---

## c5 / GOOD manufacture workforce growth

GOOD:

> “As PCG recruits for warehousing growth...”
> “each intake is larger than the last.” 

Neither is in the exhibit.

Delete both.

The only evidentially safe proposition is:

> any new hire receives an induction centred on haulage with no warehousing/CRM content.

---

## c6 manufactures alternatives

The criterion introduces:

* hiring an external warehousing leadership team;
* running the division at arm's length. 

The GOOD adds:

* separate P&L;
* acquisition capacity. 

Those are author-created options, not exhibit evidence.

You don't need them to make the criterion verdict-neutral.

Use the genuine uncertainty already present:

> four years is a material horizon and the exhibit describes present culture rather than PCG's ability to alter it over that period.

That keeps both judgements available without inventing a strategy.

---

## GOOD reintroduces the sample→population problem

The repaired c2 properly says three promotions do not prove the entire leadership population. 

But the conclusion says:

> “promotion criteria ensure haulage thinkers lead” 

No.

Use:

> the recent promotion pattern reinforces haulage experience in senior operations leadership.

---

## GOOD invents future events

> “early contracts underperform”
> “commercially skilled hires leave” 

Those should be framed as **risks**, not future facts.

---

## Fake precision

The recommendation specifies:

> tonnage moved and client satisfaction **equal weight** in bonuses. 

Why 50:50?

No exhibit evidence supports it.

Recommend incorporating relevant warehousing/customer metrics without inventing a weighting.

---

## Reveal repeats the problem

The teaching reveal says:

> “1,400 employees will rationally deprioritise warehousing clients” and early contracts will underperform / hires leave. 

So the reveal teaches precisely the evidential overreach the drill supposedly exists to prevent.

### Approval

**NO.**

The rubric is salvageable. The GOOD and reveal need to be brought back inside it.

# A3 — Formulation v implementation

## **4/10 — Definitely not publishable yet**

There are two objective reconciliation failures before getting to judgement.

### Hard arithmetic error

The scenario says:

> 94,000 users = **33%** of a 280,000 target. 

Actually:

**94,000 / 280,000 = 33.57%**

Normally rounded: **34%**.

For a conceptual drill, don't make the learner second-guess supplied arithmetic.

Change to:

> **approximately 34%**

---

### Hard date error

Timeline:

* pilot: March 2023;
* rollout: September 2023;
* survey: 18 months later = approximately March 2025. 

GOOD says the March 2023 pilot was:

> “eighteen months before the survey...” 

It was about **24 months before** the survey.

---

## c2 infers management's mental state

> “commissioning the pilot shows leaders understood ... adoption risk.” 

The exhibit proves they **tested before scaling**.

It doesn't prove exactly what they subjectively understood.

Use:

> the pilot allowed adoption risk to be identified before wider rollout.

---

## c2 invents recipients

> the internal report placed intelligence “in the hands of those who would plan the rollout.” 

Not established.

Use:

> made actionable intelligence available within the organisation.

---

## c3 — low digital literacy is invented

> “many with low digital literacy, as the 38% ... implied” 

No.

38% could result from usability, training, connectivity, confidence, language, process complexity, etc.

Delete it.

---

## c3 turns recommendation into sole mechanism

> regional intermediaries were “the mechanism through which adoption was supposed to reach 280,000 members.” 

The pilot recommended them.

It did not prove they were the exclusive delivery mechanism.

Use:

> an important implementation support explicitly identified by the pilot.

---

## c4 is fundamentally overclaimed

It says:

> “Communication governance was absent”
> regional managers are the “primary conduit”
> “there was no one positioned” to support farmers
> launch was the “moment of greatest adoption potential”
> and the later result is “directly traceable” / “post-hoc confirmation”. 

Almost all of that exceeds the exhibit.

And c6 later correctly says the case **does not isolate causation**. 

So **c4 and c6 contradict each other**.

Rebuild c4 around:

> Four of six regional managers received no advance briefing, weakening regional preparedness. The later 61% finding is consistent with inadequate on-the-ground support, but does not prove the briefing failure caused non-adoption.

---

## c5 sneaks in missing facts

It permits a formulation-based conclusion because the recommendation allegedly lacked:

* an owner;
* role design;
* launch gate. 

The exhibit establishes only that **no roles were assigned**.

It doesn't tell us what the report contained beyond the recommendation.

Safer counter-reading:

> formulation may be criticised because leadership proceeded from pilot to rollout without ensuring the pilot's implementation requirement had been converted into an owned action.

---

## c6 says “nothing else” explains the result

> “unexplained by anything else the case records” 

But the case itself records **poor regional briefing**.

So this is internally false.

---

## GOOD — “not a resource question”

> “This was not a resource question — the board had already approved KES 420 million.” 

A budget approval does not prove:

* sufficient funds remained;
* liaison posts were budgeted;
* the project wasn't overspending;
* staff could be recruited.

Delete it.

---

## GOOD — “removed from implementation plan”

Scenario:

> roles were never assigned.

GOOD:

> “Removing it from the implementation plan...” 

Those aren't equivalent.

---

## GOOD — “headline outputs”

> “were not buried in appendices; they were the pilot's headline outputs.” 

Completely invented.

Worse, the model uses that invented fact to defeat the alternative interpretation.

That is exactly the author-knowledge defect P-N3 is meant to catch.

---

## GOOD ranks causes without evidence

> “the pilot's warning is the most plausible explanation available.” 

The exhibit doesn't permit a ranking between:

* unassigned liaison roles;
* weak regional briefing;
* potentially other unreported causes.

Say:

> the outcomes are consistent with the wider implementation-support failures recorded in the case.

---

## Reveal repeats unsupported language

It calls the liaison role:

> “the delivery mechanism for 280,000 adoptions”

and says the unbriefed managers left:

> “no one positioned to champion the platform...” 

Same problem.

### Approval

**NO.**

Excellent drill concept. Current execution still teaches causation where the exhibit supports only consistency.

# A4 — Ethical threats

## **3.5/10 — Furthest from publishable**

The scenario itself is substantially stronger than the earlier version because there is now real evidence of:

* family financial interest;
* prolonged vendor relationship;
* adverse treatment immediately after a hotline report;
* actual transition costs. 

But the rubric keeps constructing facts around them.

---

## c1 invents who controls the authority limit

Criterion:

> retainer sits below the limit because Camacho “set the parameters under which the limit applies.” 

Not in exhibit.

GOOD repeats it:

> Camacho controlled “scope parameters”, so controlled whether the retainer stayed within his limit. 

Also not in exhibit.

The challenge is already strong without this:

> being below an authority threshold demonstrates formal approval authority, not freedom from conflict where the approver's spouse owns 34% of the supplier.

Use that.

---

## c1 makes a false financial assumption

> the 34% stake “rises in value as the retainer grows.” 

Not necessarily.

Revenue isn't equity value.

Safe version:

> the spouse's 34% interest gives the household a significant financial interest in DataSphere's commercial success.

---

## c1 extends the conflict back eight years without evidence

The vendor relationship started in 2017.

The spouse's stake was **disclosed in 2019**. 

We don't know when she acquired it.

Therefore:

> “every renewal and scope extension over eight years may have been distorted”

is not closed by the exhibit.

At minimum, focus scrutiny on decisions **from the point the conflict existed/disclosed**.

---

## c2 turns familiarity threat into fact

> Camacho has “almost certainly lost the critical distance...” 

No.

The relationship creates a **risk of excessive familiarity / reduced challenge**.

That's exactly why it is called a threat.

Don't convert the threat into proof.

---

# c3 contains the largest invented chain

It says:

> “the ethics hotline is one of the very rules Camacho invokes...” 

No. He invokes his **authority limit**.

It then says:

> the process that would verify his claim “was shut down before it could report”.

The exhibit does not say the hotline case stopped.

Then:

> Camacho's assertion “cannot be verified from inside CA”.

Also unsupported. Internal audit, compliance, procurement records, audit committee etc. may exist.

Then:

> CA has “no working internal means” to test the arrangement.

Again unsupported.

A very strong point exists without any of this:

* Ríos used the formal hotline;
* within one week her manager removed her from related work;
* that same manager denigrated hotline submissions.

That creates a **serious apparent intimidation/retaliation risk** and could deter future reporting.

That's enough.

# c3 and c5 contradict one another

c5 says correctly:

> don't prejudge what the investigation should find. 

GOOD says:

> “it is visible retaliation against a reporter.” 

Pick one.

The exhibit supports:

> **strong prima facie indicators of retaliation**

not a concluded finding.

---

# c5 imports legal status

> “her protected-disclosure status confirmed...” 

The case tells us only that she used an ethics hotline.

It does not establish that her complaint meets a particular Colombian statutory protected-disclosure regime.

Use:

> written assurance that she will be protected from retaliation while the matter is independently investigated.

---

# c5 invents the hotline's hierarchy

> “CA's primary internal control against future conflicts.” 

No evidence says **primary**.

Use:

> an important internal speak-up control.

---

# c5/GOOD rank legal exposure without evidence

GOOD says:

> legal/reputational exposure “will dwarf any operational inconvenience.” 

Nothing quantifies either.

That's a hidden ranking.

Use:

> could create serious legal and reputational exposure.

---

# c4 prescribes the safeguard instead of marking the safeguard act

It essentially requires:

* immediate recusal;
* retrospective VFM review. 

Those are good answers.

They should not be the **only** answers.

A candidate could reasonably recommend another package that:

1. removes the conflicted executive from decision-making; and
2. independently tests the commercial integrity of the existing relationship.

Mark that act.

Let recusal + independent VFM review be the model.

---

# c6 is still too binary

It recognises either:

* continue with safeguards;
* exit/re-tender. 

There are valid intermediate courses:

* bridge arrangement under independent oversight;
* staged tender;
* temporary continuation during competitive procurement.

Again: **mark the decision structure, not two approved labels.**

---

# GOOD changes “transition” into “operational gap”

Scenario:

> minimum six-month **transition**. 

GOOD:

> “a six-month operational gap during which CA's bid capability would be impaired.” 

Not the same.

A transition could be parallel.

Use:

> minimum six-month transition carrying migration and continuity risk.

# The reveal teaches a false ACCA marking rule

This is publication-blocking on its own.

Reveal:

> “posing questions to the reader earns no marks at all”
> “each question earns nothing at all.” 

Yet the pack itself says an identified point earns **1 mark**, with the second for development. 

And c1 itself allows 1 mark for an identified but undeveloped self-interest threat. 

The correct teaching is:

> Questions are not a substitute for professional conclusions. They are unlikely to earn development credit and weaken the professional-scepticism performance because the analytical burden is left with the reader. Where the underlying technical concern is nevertheless clearly identifiable, limited technical credit may still be available.

That is accurate and still teaches the intended lesson.

## The authoring rule I'd now add beneath P-N3

You already have the central doctrine. I'd add one operational test:

> **Every verb stronger than “suggests / risks / is consistent with” needs an exhibit fact that closes the weaker alternative.**

Words I'd automatically red-flag in model answers and rubrics:

**proves, confirms, demonstrates that X caused, ensures, prevents, no one, every, only, absent, primary, directly, certainly, must have, cannot be verified, was shut down, will fail, will leave.**

They aren't inherently wrong. But in these drills they are exactly where the author most often starts knowing more than the candidate.
