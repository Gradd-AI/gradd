# A1 — Leadership style

## **8.5/10 — DO NOT PUBLISH YET**

### Blocking issue 1 — c4 still makes a side issue too compulsory

The question is leadership style. c4 now handles the voluntary-survey problem correctly, but it requires a candidate to spend a full 2/10 marks:

* testing the CFO's statistical inference;
* specifying what non-respondents would have to look like;
* recommending a different data-collection exercise. 

That analysis is relevant to the recommendation, but the criterion goes too far when it calls the 68% figure **“load-bearing for the style recommendation”**.

It isn't necessarily load-bearing. A candidate could make a strong leadership recommendation from:

* what Episodes A–C demonstrate;
* the 340-person redeployment;
* the board's weeks-not-months constraint;

while treating the survey as inconclusive.

I wouldn't delete c4, but I would stop making its particular methodology analysis mandatory. Mark the act:

> Assess the reliability and significance of the 68% evidence, including the CFO's challenge, and explain how much weight it should receive in the leadership recommendation.

That allows the current analysis without turning it into the only correct route.

### Blocking issue 2 — GOOD still overstates Episode A

The rubric is now properly hedged: directive action **risked** sacrificing input and buy-in. 

The GOOD goes further:

> using directive leadership as a default **“builds a compliance culture that inhibits”** upward communication. 

That is stronger than the exhibit and stronger than c1.

Use:

> risks building a compliance-oriented culture which may inhibit upward challenge.

### Blocking issue 3 — GOOD promises too much from directive leadership

The model says the directive-led alternative:

> **“would meet the timetable outright”**. 

Episode A proves Liem can make one decision very quickly. It does not prove an entire 340-person restructure would meet the board's timetable.

Use:

> would better protect the board's timetable.

There's another small evidential blend in the same paragraph: the 68% survey records **role uncertainty**, not directly “buy-in”, and the VND 420bn loss isn't shown to have caused that uncertainty.

### What is now clean

c1 and c2 are properly repaired. c5 now correctly marks **the structure of the judgement**, accepts multiple styles, uses “credible material alternative” instead of a hidden ranking, and correctly limits zero marks to c5 itself rather than the whole answer. 

The BAD/hint/reveal also now line up very well: the BAD genuinely answers the adjacent change-management question, and the reveal teaches exactly that failure. 

### Call

**NO — but this is one short edit pass from approval.**

# A4 — Ethical threats

## **5.5/10 — DO NOT PUBLISH**

# Rebuilt c3 — rubric improved substantially; GOOD did not follow it

The new c3 is much safer than before:

* retaliation is an **apparent risk**, not a concluded fact;
* deterrence is framed as something that **could** happen;
* it expressly says the case does not tell us what happened to Ríos's report;
* it expressly refuses to assume CA has no other internal route. 

That is the right direction.

But it still contains two smaller overclaims:

> procurement staff are **“exactly the people best placed”** to raise the concern;

and:

> CA **“cannot rely”** on its speak-up channel while the treatment stands. 

“Best placed” is another hidden ranking. “Cannot rely” is too absolute from one incident.

Use:

> procurement staff are among those likely to possess relevant information;

and:

> the treatment materially weakens confidence that the speak-up channel will surface future concerns.

---

## Major blocker — GOOD retains the old c3 fabrication verbatim

Despite the rebuilt criterion, the GOOD still opens intimidation with:

> **“The ethics hotline is one of the rules Camacho invokes, and it is also the mechanism by which his claim would have been tested.”** 

Neither fact is in the exhibit.

Camacho invokes one thing:

> his **authority limit**. 

The hotline is a reporting channel. The exhibit doesn't say it is the investigative mechanism that tests his claim.

So the rebuilt c3 and the GOOD currently contradict each other.

**This alone blocks publication.**

Replace the opening with something like:

> Ríos used CA's formal channel to raise precisely the conflict Camacho says is within the rules. Her removal within one week, coupled with Salazar's disparaging statement about hotline reports, creates a serious apparent retaliation risk and weakens the weight the committee should place on management assurances made in that environment.

There may have been another type of board/compliance review.
