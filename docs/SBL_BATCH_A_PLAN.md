## THE RULE THAT PICKED THESE FIVE

Two conditions, both required:

1. **Zero coverage.** Section A is `0/12` in `docs/SBL_CROSSWALK_LEDGER.md` — the only section
   with no asset behind any outcome, and the one `SBL_FAILURE_CATALOGUE.md` §3 names as the
   **strongest overlap: zero coverage, recurring evidence**.
2. **An evidenced failure to build the golden BAD against.** Every drill below names a mode from
   `docs/evidence/SBL_FAILURE_CATALOGUE.md` with its report and page. The golden BAD is designed
   to commit that mode and nothing else.

### ⚠️ TWO OUTCOMES WERE EXCLUDED ON CONDITION 2, AND THE REASON IS NOT "THEY ARE SAFE"

**`A3b`** (management behaviour against the IESBA / professional-body codes) and **`A1b`**
(entrepreneurship and intrapreneurship) return **zero hits across all seven examiner reports**.
They meet condition 1 and fail condition 2: there is no observed candidate failure to author the
BAD against, so the BAD would have to be invented — and a drill that teaches against an invented
failure is exactly what the catalogue was built to prevent.

**This is NOT the same claim as "A3b is a safe topic", and the distinction is load-bearing.**
Caveat 2 of the catalogue is explicit: *a topic with zero hits is not a safe topic* — absence of
failure evidence usually means the topic was not examined in these ten sittings, and all such
outcomes remain examinable. The exclusion is about what we can evidence a BAD against **today**,
not about what a student needs. `A3b` and `A1b` stay on the build list; they need a different
authoring route (or a later sitting's evidence), not a lower priority.

*(For the record: an earlier reconstruction of this batch put `A3b` at the centre of the
scepticism drill. It was wrong for precisely this reason and the correction is why the rule above
is written down rather than assumed.)*

## STANDING RULINGS — ALL FIVE

**R1 · NO MODEL NAME IN ANY STEM.** Not in the requirement, not in an exhibit heading, not in a
character's line. The examiners record the mechanism twice (**MJ25 p.4**, **MJ26 p.4**): a noun in
the requirement summons an unrequested model and answers go to the model instead of the task —
*"change"* in the requirement produced answers about Lewin's change process model, which was not
asked for. **A stem that names the model teaches the failure it is supposed to be testing.** The
stem names the ACT and the OBJECT; choosing a tool is the candidate's job. A golden GOOD may reach
for a framework of its own accord — that is judgement being demonstrated — but it must apply it,
never explain it.

**R2 · THE RUBRIC IS THE EXAMINERS' FOUR-PART DEVELOPMENT TEST, ENCODED DIRECTLY.** Two marks are
earned only where a relevant point is identified **and then developed** by:

1. evaluating how **significant** the point is;
2. using the **information provided that relates the point directly to THIS organisation**;
3. explaining the **consequences** for the organisation;
4. supporting the point with a relevant **example from the case material**.

A point identified but left undeveloped earns **one mark**. **Every criterion is worth exactly 2
marks and states the 1-mark tier in its own `required_point`.**

⚠️ **THIS IS ACCA'S OWN SCHEME, NOT OURS.** It is published on one page of all seven reports
(catalogue **M3**), and the arithmetic — that candidates with few or no developed points had to
find more single-mark points in order to pass — is stated at **MJ25 p.4, SD25 p.4 and MJ26 p.5**.
It is encoded as a rule, not offered as a preference, for that reason.

**R3 · THE ANSWER IS A DOCUMENT FOR A NAMED READER**, and never refers to material as *"the
pre-seen"* (**MJ26 p.4** — senior leadership would not know the term).

## THE FIVE

Setting is international and never UK or Ireland, per the standing diversity rule.

### SBL-A1 · `A2b` · **analysis** · L2

> *Advise on the style of leadership appropriate to manage strategic change.*

**Scenario:** three described episodes of a **named CEO's actual behaviour** during a restructure.
**Requirement:** identify the styles in evidence and advise which fits the change now facing the
organisation.

**Designed failure — M2, the wrong object (7/7, and the examiners' #1 stated reason since MJ25).**
The golden BAD writes about **the change programme** instead of **the styles**, and reaches for a
named change model. This is MJ26's own recorded failure reproduced deliberately: **MJ26 p.4** —
change leadership *styles* were the object; answers were about the CEO's proposal, with styles
discussed at most incidentally. **MJ25 p.4** is the same shape one sitting earlier.

**Why behaviour and not a description of styles:** if the scenario labels the styles, identifying
them is reading. Three episodes of behaviour make identification an inference from evidence,
which is the `analysis` act.

### SBL-A2 · `A2d` · **evaluation** · L3

> *Assess the impact of culture on organisational purpose and strategy.*

**Scenario:** culture described entirely through **artefacts** — what gets rewarded, who gets
promoted, what meetings look like. **Requirement:** assess how that culture constrains a stated
strategic aim.

**Designed failure — M4, generic theory unapplied (7/7, with a named topic every sitting).** The
BAD describes the cultural web **as a model**, with no reference to this organisation.
**S23 p.9** (NEDs and board diversity described with no reference to the company) and **D23 p.8**
(the six capitals described, never connected to stakeholders) are the cited instances of the
identical move.

**Note the interaction with R1:** the stem must not say "cultural web". Describing culture through
artefacts is what makes the model reachable without naming it — and makes the BAD's failure a
choice the candidate made, not one the stem invited.

### SBL-A3 · `A1a` · **analysis** · L3

> *Explain the role of effective leadership and identify the key leadership traits effective in the
> successful formulation and implementation of strategy and change management.*

**Scenario:** an exhibit setting out a strategy **formulated well and implemented badly**.
**Requirement:** assess the leadership contribution to each half.

**Designed failure — M3, points identified but not developed (7/7, the highest-value mode in the
catalogue).** The BAD identifies **four correct points and develops none**, scoring the examiners'
own single-mark tier. This is the one drill where the BAD is *not wrong* — every point it makes is
right, and it still scores half. That is the lesson.

### SBL-A4 · `A3d` · **scepticism** · L3

> *Assess the nature and impact of different ethical threats and recommend appropriate safeguards
> to prevent or mitigate such threats.*

**Scenario:** a **quoted assertion from a named finance director** that an arrangement is *"within
the rules"*. **Requirement:** assess the threats it creates and recommend safeguards.

**ONE golden BAD — M7, scepticism performed as questions (3/7: J24 p.19, SD25 p.9, MJ26 p.9).**
Weaker candidates try to show scepticism by asking the reader questions instead of giving an
assessment; **MJ26 p.9 states that no marks will be awarded for it.** The BAD poses questions and
commits to nothing.

⚠️ **M8's AGGRESSIVE POLE IS A NAMED CONTRAST IN THE `full_reveal`, NOT A SECOND GOLDEN BAD.**
Tone fails in **both** directions — **SD25 p.9** (calling named directors incompetent is not an
appropriate way to raise a concern) and **MJ26 p.14** (accusations of fraud that neither the
pre-seen nor the exhibit justified, described as too extreme a reaction, with a warning not to
antagonise the recipient). A rubric that only penalises insufficient challenge teaches the MJ26
failure. Carrying it as prose in the reveal rather than as a second BAD keeps the deterministic
BAD/GOOD separation single-axis, which is what N4 can actually verify.

### SBL-A5 · `A3a` · **evaluation** · L3

> *Critically evaluate the concept of responsible leadership and the creation of public value by
> acting in the public interest.*

**Scenario:** a decision with a **defensible commercial case and a public-interest cost**.
**Requirement:** evaluate and commit to a verdict.

**Designed failure — M9 fence-sitting (1/7, S23 p.16) + M1 copy-paste (7/7), the latter
detector-caught.** The BAD lifts the exhibit's own justification **verbatim** and never lands.

⚠️ **M9 IS A SINGLE-SITTING MODE and the catalogue says "do not build a drill against this
alone".** It is not alone here: M1 is 7/7 and is the deterministically detected half (F1 by
`scenarioCopyOverlap` / `longestVerbatimRun`), with fence-sitting as F4. Both are raised without
the model layer. **S23 p.16** also records that candidates who *did* commit scored well, and that
recommending immediate acceptance without evaluating weaknesses was itself poor judgement — so
the GOOD must weigh before it lands, not merely land.

⚠️ **N6a MEASURES LABELLING, NOT DEMAND**, and for `analysis` / `evaluation` the mode it counts

corpus as a generic "this criterion is where the skill is marked" marker — the live AFM rows label
it on `communication` criteria too. Never report an N6a share as act coverage.

### What the six gates do NOT cover here

**There is no numeric verifier on this pipeline**, and on AFM that cost a drill (D7 passed all six
gates asserting the opposite of its own figures). None of these five gives the candidate a
derivable chain — every figure is a stated given — which is the structural answer, not a promise
that the gates would have caught one.

## OPEN, DELIBERATELY

1. **No area-entry ranks.** `pickEntryDrill` keys on the `model_answer` heading and orders drills
   within an area for the zero-attempt serve. **Nothing serves SBL** — no route, no entitlement, no
   price — so a rank would be ordering an empty queue. Owed before the first SBL serve, not before
   the first SBL row.
2. **Per-requirement PS marking is unbuilt** (`AFM_SURFACED.md` item 6). These are drills, not case
   requirements, so they do not need it — but an SBL *case* will, and it ships with its own band
   matrix under P-M1 or not at all.
3. **`A3b` and `A1b` still need a route.** See the exclusion note above; they are deferred for want
   of evidenced failure modes, not deprioritised.
