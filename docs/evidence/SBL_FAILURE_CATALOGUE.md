# SBL failure catalogue — the evidence base for every SBL drill

**This decides what each SBL drill teaches against.** It is the SBL analogue of
`AFM_NARRATIVE_EVIDENCE.md` §1b, and it follows the same rule: a mode is only in here if it can
be pointed at a report and a page.

---

## PROVENANCE — read this before using a single line below

**VERIFIED 2026-08-18, ALL SEVEN REPORTS, PAGE BY PAGE.** Every report was extracted with
`pdftotext -raw`, split on form feeds, and searched page by page. **Page alignment was calibrated
BEFORE any claim was checked** — four independently-known citations (MJ26 p.8, p.9, p.3 and a

**WORDING POSTURE: PARAPHRASE, NOT QUOTATION.** The reports are ACCA copyright and are

## ⚠️ TWO CAVEATS THAT GOVERN EVERY USE OF THIS FILE

**1. THIS IS OCCURRENCE-COUNTED, NOT MARKS-WEIGHTED.** "7/7" means a mode was stated in seven
reports. It does NOT mean it carries seven times the marks of a mode stated once. SBL is one
100-mark paper of three tasks; the reports do not publish a per-mode mark impact, and nothing here
derives one. **Reading this file as a marks priority repeats exactly the error the superseded 35%
coverage headline made.**

**2. A TOPIC WITH ZERO HITS IS NOT A SAFE TOPIC.** Absence of failure evidence is not evidence of
safety — it usually means the topic was not examined in these ten sittings. Searched across all
seven reports and returning **nothing**: public-sector governance (B6), Johnson Scholes and
Whittington (C1b), the resource-based view / competences (C4), risk registers and heat maps
(D2b). **All four are examinable.** Do not read their absence as a reason to deprioritise them,
and never cite this file as evidence that they are low-risk.

## §2 — THE MODES

### M1 · Copy-paste or paraphrase with no value added — **7/7**
Present in every report's stated reasons (R4) and discussed in the body of each.
The sharpest instances, both usable as designed BAD answers:
- **MJ26 p.8** — candidates pasted the internal auditor's own words without even changing the
  first-person pronoun to name the auditor.
- **SD25 p.8** — candidates pasted "our"/"we" from an exhibit that spoke for the OTHER company,
  not the client.

**It got WORSE, not better, after the pre-seen was introduced** — S23 p.7 records more copy-paste
than previous sittings and D23 p.6 more again; J24 p.7 repeats it.

### M2 · Answering the adjacent question — not the task as set — **7/7**
R1 in every report. The mechanism is named precisely and is drillable: the candidate misses **the
object of the requirement verb** and answers about the adjacent noun instead.
- **MJ25 p.4** — contextual features were the object of the verb; answers were about the CEO's
  proposed programme of changes instead.
- **MJ26 p.4** — change leadership *styles* were the object; answers were about the CEO's proposal,
  with styles discussed at most incidentally.
- **SD25 p.4** — asked how risks could be mitigated, answered what the risks were; asked for power
  and interest *in the acquisition*, answered about routine operations.

**Secondary trigger, twice (MJ25 p.4, MJ26 p.4):** a noun in the requirement summons an
unrequested model — "change" in the requirement produced answers about Lewin's change process
model, which was not asked for.

**S23 p.5** states the general form memorably: candidates answer the task they *wish* had been
set rather than the task actually set.

> ⚠️ **CORRECTION (2026-08-18).** The superseded catalogue claimed this mode was *"named as a
> top-two reason for failure in every one"*. **That is false — it is top-two in 3 of 7.** See the
> re-ordering note in §1. The mode's 7/7 presence is correct; only the prominence claim was wrong.

### M3 · Points identified but not developed — the two-mark rule — **7/7**
`S23 p.8 · D23 p.7 · J24 p.7 · D24 p.7 · MJ25 p.7 · SD25 p.8 · MJ26 p.8`

**THE HIGHEST-VALUE MODE IN THE CATALOGUE, because the examiners publish the rubric themselves**,
on one page of all seven reports. Two marks are awarded only where a relevant point is
identified/explained AND THEN developed by:

1. evaluating how significant the point is;
2. using the information provided that relates the point directly to the organisation;
3. explaining the consequences for the organisation;
4. supporting the point with relevant examples from the case material.

⚠️ **EARLY SITTINGS SUBSTITUTE THE CASE COMPANY'S NAME** where later ones say "the organisation"
(S23 names Corjetz in parts 2 and 3). A search for the later wording alone reports the test as
missing from the early reports; it is not. Verified company-agnostically in all seven.

The arithmetic is stated at **MJ25 p.4, SD25 p.4 and MJ26 p.5**: with few or no developed points,
candidates had to find more single-mark points in order to pass.

### M4 · Generic theory, unapplied — **7/7, with a named topic every sitting**
Each verified on its cited page:
- **S23 p.9** — NEDs and board diversity described with no reference to the company.
- **D23 p.8** — the six capitals of Integrated Reporting described, never connected to stakeholders.
- **J24 p.8** — CSR discussed in general rather than focusing on stakeholder impact.
- **D24 p.8** — general risks and benefits of cloud computing with no application to the company.
- **MJ25 p.8** — e-marketing methods described without showing how they would improve stakeholder relations.
- **SD25 p.8** — generic advantages of debt or equity financing.
- **MJ26 p.8** — generic advantages of change leadership styles, unapplied to the change at hand.

Every report closes the passage with the same instruction: answers must not merely repeat syllabus
knowledge or theory without applying it to the exam context.

### M5 · Calculation without interpretation — **2/7, NOT 5/7**
Stated as a zero-credit rule at **S23 p.20** (no marks for calculations alone) and **MJ26 p.11**
(the finance director could already see whether a figure rose or fell).

> ⚠️ **CORRECTION (2026-08-18).** The superseded catalogue cited this to five reports —
> S23, D24, MJ25, SD25, MJ26. **D24 p.7 contains no calculation, interpretation or figures
> language at all**, and MJ25 p.4 and SD25 p.4 carry nothing on this mode either. Three of the
> five citations were unsourceable. The mode is real and it is only in two reports.

**Two specific technical errors, both MJ26 p.12 and both single-sitting:** confusing profit margin
with profit (comparing a % change in profit against a target margin — profit margin is assumed
knowledge at Strategic Professional level), and mixing up the two comparison years so that
improving performance read as worsening.

### M6 · Pre-seen misuse — three distinct sub-modes, do not merge them
**(a) Pre-prepared answers — 3/7.** `S23 p.5` (answers pre-prepared from the pre-seen and then
used regardless of the question asked) · `D23 p.4` (rote-learnt answers based solely on the
pre-seen) · **`MJ26 p.3`, the sharpest and most recent:** issues flagged in online forums and
public reviews of the June 2026 pre-seen were written into answers despite being irrelevant to
the task.

> ⚠️ **CORRECTION (2026-08-18).** The superseded catalogue headed this sub-mode
> *"QUESTION-SPOTTING AND PRE-PREPARED ANSWERS"*. **"Question-spotting" appears nowhere in any of
> the seven reports** — it is our label sitting in a heading that reads as citation. The
> behaviour is sourced; the phrase is not. Removed.

**(b) Forcing pre-seen detail in regardless of relevance — 3/7.** `J24 p.4` (a pre-seen
demographic statistic written into answers to all tasks irrespective of relevance) · `S23 p.20`
(exhibit ignored, results explained from the pre-seen) · `D23 p.4`.

**(c) Naming the pre-seen in the answer — 1/7, NEW at `MJ26 p.4`.** Candidates should not refer to
the pre-seen by name: it undermines the professional tone of a document addressed to senior
leadership, who would not know the term. **Directly relevant to how a familiarisation product
should teach.**

### M7 · Scepticism performed as questions rather than committed assessment — **3/7**
`J24 p.19 · SD25 p.9 · MJ26 p.9`

Weaker candidates try to show scepticism by asking the reader questions instead of giving an
assessment. The examiners state that this is not how scepticism is demonstrated, that it weakens
the answer, and — **at MJ26 p.9 — that no marks will be awarded for it**. Both J24 p.19 and
MJ26 p.9 address tutors directly.

> ⚠️ **CORRECTION (2026-08-18), UPWARD.** The superseded catalogue called this *"two consecutive
> reports"* (SD25, MJ26). It is **three, spanning two years** — J24 p.19 states it too. That moves
> the mode from a recent development to a recurring habit, and strengthens the case for building
> against it.

Maps directly onto the existing **N5 committed-verdict gate** and the **F4** disqualifier.

### M8 · Tone and register — and it fails in BOTH directions — **3/7 for the aggressive half**
- **Too deferential:** `S23 p.16` — points made in favour of a proposal throughout, with no
  recognition of its weaknesses; an unprofessional tone when challenge *was* attempted.
- **Too aggressive:** `SD25 p.9` — calling named directors incompetent is not an appropriate way
  to raise a concern with them · `MJ25 p.13` · `MJ26 p.14` — accusations of fraud that neither the
  pre-seen nor the exhibit justified, described as too extreme a reaction, with a warning not to
  antagonise the recipient.

⚠️ **A RUBRIC THAT ONLY PENALISES INSUFFICIENT CHALLENGE WILL TEACH THE MJ26 FAILURE.** Any
scepticism drill must carry both disqualifiers.

### M9 · Fence-sitting — no committed recommendation — **1/7**
`S23 p.16` only. Candidates who failed to give a final recommendation, or sat on the fence; the
same page notes that candidates who *did* commit scored well, and that recommending immediate
acceptance without evaluating weaknesses was itself poor professional judgement.
**Single-sitting — do not build a drill against this alone.**

### M10 · Format not matched to the named recipient — **body-discussed in all 7**
`S23 p.13` (slides requested; presentation notes missing or merged into the slides) ·
`D23 p.8` (a board of mixed specialisms, so the presentation had to assume limited knowledge).

⚠️ **`MJ26 p.13` — PRESENTATION FORMAT COST AN *ANALYSIS* MARK, NOT A COMMUNICATION MARK.** Answers
given as tables or bullet points limited the depth and breadth of the analysis. **The skills are
not cleanly separable in the way the tags imply.**

### M11 · Not using all exhibits / not linking across them — **6/7**
Every report except D24 states that material from more than one exhibit may be relevant, and names
that sitting's pairing: `S23 p.6 · D23 p.5 · J24 p.5 · MJ25 p.5 · SD25 p.5 · MJ26 p.5`.
**`MJ26 p.13`** makes the link an explicit PS driver — analysis marks were frequently driven by how
far candidates discussed the links between the information in one exhibit. **`MJ26 p.11`** gives
the failure in full: results and KPIs considered in isolation, with no comment on the links
between them.

### M12 · Doing more than the requirement asked — **4/7**
`J24 · MJ25 p.5 · SD25 p.6 · MJ26 p.7`. SD25 p.6 states the governing rule cleanly: task
requirements may refer directly to exhibits, so **the exhibits define what answers should cover —
no more and no less**.

### M13 · Poor command of English — **2/7, AND IT DISAPPEARED**
`D23 p.4 · J24 p.4` only. **Searched across all seven reports and absent from D24, MJ25, SD25 and
MJ26** — the earlier verification flagged this as an unproven negative; it is now proven.
**Report the disappearance; do not build for it.**

### M14 · Time allocation — improved, and the allowance MOVED — **7/7**
Every report publishes a marks→minutes guide. The recommended reading-and-planning allowance
changed between sittings:
- **15 minutes** — `D23 p.5 · J24 p.6 · D24 p.5`
- **30 minutes** — `MJ25 pp.5–6 · SD25 pp.5–6 · MJ26 pp.5–6`

`D23 p.5` records a significant minority failing to finish Task 3. By MJ25 onward the examiners
report no significant time problem. **Time management is the one mode that has measurably
improved**, and R10 correspondingly drops out of the stated reasons at MJ26.

## §4 — WHAT THIS FILE DOES NOT SETTLE

1. **No mark weighting.** See caveat 1. Nothing here says what a mode costs.
2. **Ten sittings is the whole corpus.** Seven reports covering ten sittings is what exists; a mode
   absent from it may simply not have been examined.
3. **The modes are not independent.** M1, M2, M3 and M4 co-occur in the examiners' own prose —
   an answer that copies an exhibit is usually also undeveloped and unapplied. Counting them as
   four separate failures over-counts the underlying behaviour.
4. **M10 shows the professional-skill tags are leaky.** MJ26 p.13 has a presentation-format failure
   costing an ANALYSIS mark. Any design that assumes one failure maps to one skill tag is already
   contradicted by the evidence.
