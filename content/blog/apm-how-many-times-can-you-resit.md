You failed APM. You can book it again. So the first question is usually the practical one.

How many times can you resit?

## The honest answer

There is no fixed attempt limit for APM itself. If you fail, you can enter a later sitting and try again.

APM runs four times a year — in March, June, September and December — so the next attempt is rarely more than a few months away.

One time limit does exist, but it isn't about attempts: once you pass your first Strategic Professional exam, you have seven years to complete all the Strategic Professional exams. Passes older than seven years expire and must be retaken. Your Applied Knowledge and Applied Skills passes never expire.

So attempts are not the constraint.

That is the good news. It is also the trap.

## The resit trap

Because you can simply book again, the natural plan is: revise harder, sit again.

But APM's pass rate is consistently around 40% — among the lowest pass rates in the ACCA qualification — so simply booking another sitting is not a strategy. If the first fail was about technique rather than knowledge, preparing the same way again changes nothing.

You walk back in with a fuller memory of the models and the same habit of describing them.

## Why the same preparation gives the same result

### You revise content, but the exam tests application

Re-reading the notes builds recognition. APM asks you to use the models on a specific business, under time pressure. Those are different skills, and only one of them was the problem.

### You measure progress by recall, not by the marks

"I know EVA now" is not the same as "I can earn the EVA marks." The second needs an answer that interprets, challenges and recommends — none of which a content review tests.

### You practise by reading answers, not writing them

Reading a model answer feels like studying. But the exam is a writing task against a clock, and the only practice that prepares you for it is writing against a clock.

## What a resit actually has to change

Not more content. Different practice.

Write full answers to real scenarios, timed.

Force every paragraph to do something: apply the model, challenge a claim, or make a judgement.

Mark your work against the professional-skills descriptors, not a content checklist.

And before you book the next sitting, work out why you lost the marks last time — describing instead of applying, accepting instead of challenging, over-calculating, or running out of time — and drill that specific habit.

## Diagnose before you resit

The candidates who break the cycle treat the last fail as information, not just a setback.

A resit built on "I'll revise harder" repeats the exam that failed.

A resit built on "here is the exact habit that cost me, and here is how I am fixing it" is a different exam.
