Your mock came back at 55.

The real exam came back at 44.

Same subject.

Same candidate.

Different result.

That gap feels random.

It usually is not.

A common reason is marking.

Not because mock markers are useless. Not because ACCA is unfair.

Because a mock can reward the content you recognise, while the real exam rewards what the answer actually does.

Those are not the same thing.

## Self-marking rewards recognition

When you mark your own mock, you tend to look for visible content.

Did I name the model?

Did I do the calculation?

Did I reach a conclusion?

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

That can feel like 55.

But recognition is generous.

You know what you meant.

The marker can only credit what is on the page.

APM is especially dangerous here because twenty marks sit in professional skills: communication, analysis and evaluation, scepticism, and commercial acumen.

Those marks do not appear just because the model is named.

They appear when the answer is structured, applied, challenging and commercially useful.

## The same answer, two lenses

Take one paragraph from an answer about a struggling service business.

> The Building Block Model has three parts: dimensions, standards and rewards. The business should measure the right dimensions, set fair standards, and link rewards to them.

Self-marking says:

> I covered dimensions, standards and rewards. That is the whole model.

Descriptor-style marking says something different.

The answer has not investigated the specific issue.

It has not weighed evidence.

It has not applied judgement to this organisation.

It is accurate knowledge, but it is floating above the case.

Same words.

One lens says:

> Done.

The other says:

> You have not started applying it yet.

## The descriptors you did not target

The four professional skills ask for things a content checklist can miss.

**Communication** means the answer is structured for the person named in the requirement. A board paper should not read like a student brain-dump.

**Analysis and evaluation** means you develop the point, explain the consequence, weigh it, and make a judgement.

**Scepticism** means you challenge weak claims, forecasts and assumptions instead of repeating them as fact.

**Commercial acumen** means the recommendation would actually work for the organisation, given its cost, capacity, risk and context.

None of those is a fact you can simply recall.

So if your mock marking only checked content, it may have missed the fifth of the paper that APM is explicitly trying to test.

## Why mocks can flatter

A mock can flatter for several reasons.

You marked it yourself.

The marking scheme was too generous.

The feedback focused on knowledge coverage.

You gave yourself credit for what you meant.

You recognised the model and assumed the application was there.

Then the real exam marked the answer more strictly.

Not the intention.

The answer.

That is the gap.

## How to mark your mock more like APM

### 1. Hide the names

Cover the company name.

Cover the model name.

Now read the paragraph again.

If it still says something specific and useful, it is probably applied.

If it could fit almost any company, it is probably describe-only.

### 2. Find the skill before you award the mark

Before you give yourself credit, point to the skill on the page.

Where did you challenge a figure?

Where did you weigh a trade-off?

Where did you make a judgement?

Where did you write in a way the named audience could use?

If you cannot point to it, do not assume the mark is there.

### 3. Mark down, not up

If you are unsure whether a point earned the skill, be strict.

The real marker can only credit what is written.

Marking your mock softly is how an answer that feels like 55 turns into 44.
